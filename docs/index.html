te-araroa-data

The Te Araroa Trust publishes its trail data as a KMZ that almost nothing can read. This turns it into an ordinary GPX in walking order — with elevation, huts, campsites, and a resupply plan the official data does not contain.

2026-27 release (v45) built 8 September 2026 Source on GitHub

This is a snapshot, and it may already be out of date.

The trust reroutes the trail continuously and republishes each season. Nothing here updates itself, and there is no promise that it will be updated at all. This is not official and not endorsed by the Te Araroa Trust.

Before you walk, get the current files from teararoa.org.nz and read the trust's own trail notes and closure notices. Hut, water and resupply details decay faster than the route does — treat all of them as a starting point for your own checking, not as something to rely on in the field.

Why it exists

Both official files are public, and the GPX — the one most tools reach for — is the weaker of the two:

official GPXofficial KMZ
Track points28,02735,987
Elevationnoneevery vertex
Track orderalphabeticalchainage
Huts / campsitesnone184, with attributes
Section namesnoneevery segment
Bypasses / no-campingnone13 + 353

So the build reads the KMZ, uses the GPX only to check its work, and writes ordinary GPX and CSV. It then projects all 3,058 official km markers onto the assembled route: mean error 8 m, which is to say the interpolated chainage reproduces the trust's own markers essentially exactly.

The official data also contains no resupply at all — no towns, shops or supermarkets anywhere in the KMZ. 82 resupply points were researched by hand to fill that gap.

What you might build on it

The waypoints carry a GPX <type>, so a hut arrives as a hut rather than as a string to guess at. Every site has an official trail km, which makes "what is between km 1,200 and km 1,340" a filter rather than a geometry problem. Sensible things to do with that: itinerary planners that schedule by hut rather than by distance, resupply calculators, elevation profiles per section, or simply loading the GPX into a watch that refused the KMZ.

Corrections to the resupply data are the most useful contribution anyone can make — that file is hand-built and it decays. Open an issue.
